Coverage of critical positions is described not by one indicator but by three,
and they cannot be substituted for one another.

## Coverage

The share of critical positions with at least one successor. It answers the
question "where is there nothing at all". The indicator is crude: it does not
distinguish a position with one unprepared candidate from a position with three
prepared ones.

## Bench strength

The average number of successors per critical position. It answers the
question "is there enough backup if the first candidate declines". Declining is
a common event: a candidate may not want to relocate, may receive an outside
offer or may leave.

## Ready now

The share of positions with a successor ready to take over now. It answers the
question "what happens if someone leaves tomorrow".

## Why all three are needed

An organization with one hundred percent coverage and zero ready-now readiness
looks fine right up to the first resignation letter: there are plans, but no
ready people. The opposite case — high ready-now readiness with low coverage —
means the wrong positions have been prepared for.
