The position match profile is calculated against the requirements of the
competency model. If some requirements are not confirmed by any rating, the
system has two options: treat what has not been assessed as zero, or exclude it
from the calculation. Both distort the picture if left unstated.

## Zero instead of absence

Substituting zero turns a gap in data into a verdict: an employee who was not
assessed on three competencies out of ten gets a match half as high as the
actual one. Worse, the difference between "assessed and weak" and "not
assessed" disappears — and these are fundamentally different cases requiring
different actions.

## Exclusion without disclosure

Excluding unassessed requirements is more honest but creates another trap: an
employee assessed on one competency out of ten who scored high on it ends up at
the top of the ranking. The ranking starts rewarding incomplete assessment.

## Solution

The coverage indicator is shown next to the result and stored with it. An
eighty percent match with one hundred percent coverage and an eighty percent
match with thirty percent coverage are different statements, and the reader
must be able to tell them apart without asking.

> The same rule applies to the composite readiness index: the share of weight
> backed by data is stored with every calculation.
