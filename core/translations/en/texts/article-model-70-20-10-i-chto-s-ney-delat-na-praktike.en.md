The model states that an adult employee's development comes roughly seventy
percent from experience of solving work problems, twenty percent from
interaction with more experienced colleagues and ten percent from formal
training. The proportion is approximate and has been disputed in detail many
times, but the order of magnitude is consistently confirmed in practice.

## What follows from it

A development plan made up of courses works at ten percent of its possible
strength. The practical conclusion is simple: first choose stretch assignments
and rotations, then a mentor, and only then training that closes a knowledge
gap without which the assignment cannot be done.

## How the system expresses this

Each activity type has a weight reflecting its contribution to readiness.
Secondment and rotation weigh five, project work four, stretch assignments and
mentoring three, training and courses two, conferences and self-study one. Plan
completion is calculated by weight, not by the number of items.

The effect is noticeable: five completed webinars do not close a plan that
contains an unfinished secondment. That is exactly the point — easy items
should not create the appearance of progress.

## Caveat

The proportion is not dogma. For competencies that require systematic
knowledge — financial analysis, law, occupational safety — the share of formal
training is naturally higher. The model sets a default, not a prohibition.
