A position is called critical if its vacancy creates a risk to the
organization's operations. The definition is correct but impractical: too much
falls under it. In practice, criticality is assessed by two independent values.

## Impact of an unfilled vacancy

What happens if the position stays vacant for three months. Does the business
line stop, is unique knowledge lost, are commitments to the customer broken.
Rated on a scale from one to five.

## Likelihood of vacancy

How likely the position is to become vacant within the planning horizon. The
age and tenure of the current holder, their market demand, signs of
dissatisfaction and transfer plans are taken into account.

## Priority

Planning priority is the product of criticality and risk. This leads to a
non-obvious conclusion: a position of criticality three with high risk is more
urgent than a position of criticality five with a stable incumbent. A list
sorted by criticality alone systematically postpones work on the positions that
will become vacant first.

## Review

The risk assessment becomes outdated faster than the criticality assessment.
The former is best reviewed annually, the latter when the structure or strategy
changes.
