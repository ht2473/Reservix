360° feedback collects opinions about an employee from the manager, peers,
direct reports and the employee. The method answers how the employee's
behavior is perceived by those around them. It does not answer what results
the employee achieves — and that is not a flaw of the method but its limit.

## What the method shows well

* The gap between self-assessment and others' ratings. This is most often the
  subject of a developmental conversation.
* Differences in perception from above and below: an employee rated highly by
  the manager and low by direct reports is usually managing impressions rather
  than people.
* Competencies that show up in interaction: communication, collaboration,
  influence. There is no test to measure them.

## Where the method fails

First, anonymity. Without it, direct reports overrate their manager; with it,
anonymity in small groups is fictional, and this must be acknowledged openly.
Second, respondent weights. A manager's rating and a peer's rating do not carry
equal weight, and averaging without weights mixes judgments of different
nature. Third, the scale. Without levels described through observable behavior,
the procedure degenerates into a likeability scale.

## Practical conclusion

360° results are best used as one component of competency assessment, not as an
independent basis for a staffing decision. Making appointments depend on
popularity is a sure way to get managers who avoid unpopular decisions.
