Any composite indicator depends on its weights. The question is not whether it
does but how strongly: a ranking that flips with a small change in weights
cannot serve as the basis for a staffing decision.

## How it is checked

Weights are varied one at a time within set limits, the calculation is
repeated, and the resulting orders are compared with the original. The measure
is the rank correlation coefficient and the overlap of the top ten — the part of
the list from which the choice is actually made.

## What the results mean

High correlation with an almost identical top means that the choice of weighting
scheme does not affect the decision: the candidates differ enough for the order
to hold. Low correlation means the candidates are close and the decision is
made not by the data but by the model settings; in that case it is more honest
to admit that the candidates are comparable and leave the choice to the
committee.

## What the analysis shows in practice

The least robust weight is usually competency match — it is both the largest
and the most sensitive to assessment coverage. This points not to a flaw in the
model but to where effort should go: assessment coverage affects the result
more than any debate about weights.
