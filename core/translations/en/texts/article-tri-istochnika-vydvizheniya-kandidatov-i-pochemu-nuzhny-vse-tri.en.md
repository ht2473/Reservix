A candidate enters the talent pool in one of three ways: nominated by the line
manager, selected by HR based on assessment data, or self-nominated. It is
tempting to keep only one route — it simplifies the procedure — but each source
sees what the others do not.

## What the manager sees

Actual work: how a person behaves in an emergency, how they make decisions with
insufficient data, how they handle rejection. No assessment procedure measures
this. The weakness of this source is a narrow horizon: the manager compares the
employee with their own unit, not with the organization.

## What HR sees

Comparability. Assessment results are brought to a common scale, and an
employee from a branch can be compared with one from the head office. The
weakness is that assessment measures behavior shown under set conditions, not
behavior in general.

## What the employee sees

Their own intentions. The organization systematically does not know which
employees are ready to move, relocate or change direction. Self-nomination is
the only way to find out, and it also filters out the case where a prepared
candidate turns down the appointment at the last moment.

## What this means for the procedure

All three routes lead to the same thing — a nomination that goes through
approval. The source is kept in the record itself: it explains the composition
of the talent pool and makes it possible to check whether selection has turned
into a single channel.
