In many organizations mentoring exists as an attribute: a development activity
lists a mentor, and that is all. Such a record cannot answer a single
meaningful question: how many people the mentor supports, how often they meet,
how the support ended.

## What turns mentoring into a process

A mentor–mentee pair with its own term, goal and result. The goal is stated in
terms of the result: not "support" but "bring the mentee to handling the target
position's tasks independently". The meeting cadence is set when the pair is
created — the next meeting date is calculated from it, and a pair without a
cadence stops reminding anyone of itself.

## Mentor workload

The number of mentees is not a measure of work. A mentor with eight pairs and
none brought to a result does worse than a mentor with two completed ones.
That is why the workload summary shows the number of active pairs, the number
of completed ones and the average effectiveness rating side by side.

## Mentee feedback

The mentee's rating of usefulness is collected separately from HR's rating.
The two often differ, and the difference is meaningful: it shows the gap
between formally playing the role and actually helping.
